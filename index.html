Optical Sky Atlas

Look up stars, clusters, nebulae and galaxies you can observe with eyes, binoculars or a telescope. Search the catalog, plot objects on a sky chart, and use the calculators for your observing projects.

Each dot is an object; bigger and brighter means lower magnitude. Right ascension runs from 24h on the left to 0h on the right, the way a sky chart is read. Tap a dot to open it in the catalog.

Absolute magnitude

M = m − 5 log₁₀(d / 10 pc). Ignores dust dimming.

Telescope limits

Limiting magnitude ≈ 7.5 + 5 log₁₀(aperture in cm), under a dark sky. Light grasp compares to a 7 mm eye pupil.

Angular size

Full Moon is about 31 arcminutes wide. Defaults: Orion Nebula core.

Best month to observe

Month when an object is highest around local midnight. Approximate.

Catalog values are rounded from standard references (Messier and bright-star lists); distances and magnitudes vary by source, and variable stars change. Cross-check with SIMBAD or VizieR before citing in coursework.
